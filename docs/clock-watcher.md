Checking the clock in bed makes it take longer to fall asleep. Even for good sleepers.

You just want to know how much sleep is left. But in a study of 60 people, clock-watchers worried more and took longer to fall asleep. [small on-screen citation: Tang et al., 2007]

Each glance starts a countdown. Six hours left. Five and a half. That countdown keeps your brain on.

So turn your clock to face the wall, and your phone face down. Your alarm still works.

## Decisions (Oct 30)
- Direction B+C: the clock's light finds you and every glance drops a note of sleep math into it; turned to the wall,
  the light leaves you and the notes drift off; the light on the wall grows into the quiz.
- Deep blue photo (`photos.blueSky`); the seeded pick (purple Milky Way) has no file.
- Digital alarm clock seen from above, red LED light straight at your head, time in red LED digits turned to face the
  sleeper (side readout, not upright). 1:00 → 1:30 against a 7:00 alarm = "six hours left… five and a half".
- Hook: tossing and turning (head rolls across the pillow, pillow shifts, duvet folds), sideways only.
- Cover 3a: "Can't stop checking the clock at night?" over the bed with the three notes in the red light.
- Open: "Even for good sleepers" may not match Tang et al. 2007 (sample may have been people with insomnia); user kept the VO.
