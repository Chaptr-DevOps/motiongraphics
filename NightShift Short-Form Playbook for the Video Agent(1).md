# NightShift Short-Form Playbook for the Video Agent

Oct 13, 2026 · @Nick Chaptr

## The goal

Every video exists to send the right people to the NightShift sleep-check quiz. We want viewers who actually struggle with sleep and will act on what they learn, not the biggest possible audience.

- **Who we're talking to:** adults who lie awake, wake in the night, or dread bedtime. Many already own a tracker and have tried the usual tips.
- **What success looks like:** sends, saves and thoughtful comments (questions, "I tried this"), then quiz starts. Views alone don't count. Huge viral reach brings people who never sign up.
- **Where viewers go:** the caption points to the quiz, "What's keeping you awake? A 2-minute sleep check." The quiz names their pattern, shows their sleep gap and gives one thing to try tonight. The waitlist signup happens at the end of the quiz.
- **How we work:** faceless, no talking head and no expert on camera. Three to five posts a day, so formats must be repeatable and cheap to vary.
- **What we stand on:** CBT-I, the first treatment doctors recommend for long-term insomnia (American College of Physicians, 2016). Most viewers have never heard of it.

### Patterns

1. **A named technique travels.** "4-7-8 breathing" and "cognitive shuffling" are easy to pass on. Cognitive shuffling appears in three of the 17.
2. **Try it with me beats telling.** The counted breathing in video 1 and the in-bed demo in video 7 let viewers do it while watching.
3. **One line of why makes it believable.** Each technique came with a short mechanism (busy-thought loop, heart rate) or an analogy (a shaken snow globe, losing your appetite halfway through dinner).
4. **New information wins high-quality comments.** Saying the fix is in the morning (10), or that sleep hygiene alone doesn't fix chronic insomnia (15), made people argue, ask and share. Video 15's "CBT-I, did you know that was a thing?" shows how unknown CBT-I is.
5. **A specific question earns real replies.** "What time do you normally go outside in the morning?" (10) drew high-quality comments. "Comment SLEEP" (3, 6) drew volume and junk.
6. **Series build return viewers.** "Day 3 of 7" and "part 4" make people look for the rest.
7. **Relatable memes reach, but don't convert.** Videos 5, 8, 11, 12 and 16 hit 8 to 12 million views with mostly low-quality comments, often selling cortisol guides. Use the relatable moment as a hook, then teach something.
8. **Reassurance lowers sleep anxiety.** "Even if you don't sleep, you'll be fine tomorrow; your body self-corrects" (2) is the kind of line that gets saved.
9. **The situation is in the first words.** "Can't sleep? Try this," "waking up at 2 or 3 a.m.," "Insomnia is not what you think." The viewer knows in a second whether it's for them.

## Hooks and script structure

Every script follows the house structure: hook, transition, body, fix, loop line. At about 140 words a minute, a 45-second video is roughly 105 words.

| Part | Job | What works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook (first 3 seconds) | Name the viewer's situation and promise something new | A specific moment ("If you wake up at 3 a.m."), or a belief turned over ("The best sleep fix isn't at night") |
| Transition | Keep them past second three | A short promise of a payoff ("The fix is free"). Never bait twice |
| Body | The why, built with "but" and "therefore" | Cause and effect in the right order. Every line adds something new |
| Fix | One concrete step | Small, specific, doable tonight |
| Loop line | Ends so it flows back into the hook | No sign-off, no "follow for more," no share ask |

On-screen text carries the hook as a caption in the first frame, since many people watch muted. Keep it to seven words or fewer, per the motion brief.

### Lessons from our own first drafts

- Don't attack what viewers pay for, like trackers or mattresses. Show a better way to use them.
- Don't build on a premise that's only true for some people.
- Don't tell viewers their problem is common or quote stats that confirm what they already feel.
- Don't tie a hook to a day of the week.
- Never make the fix more work for a worse result, and never take away something people love (like weekend sleep-ins). Shrink it instead.
- Be concrete. "Go back when you're sleepy" is vague; "go back when your eyes feel heavy" is usable.
- Never tell people to check the clock at night. Naming a time in the hook ("3 a.m.") is fine.

## The six faceless formats

Rotate these six so three to five posts a day stay varied. Guided techniques and myth-versus-research should make up most of the mix, because they earn sends and good comments.

| Format | Length | What it is | Built from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guided technique | 45–60 s | Voiceover walks the viewer through a named technique in real time, with on-screen counts or prompts | Videos 1, 6, 7, 9 |
| Myth vs research | 20–40 s | A common belief, then what the research says, then one fix | Videos 10, 15 |
| Why it happens | 30–45 s | Explains one night-time experience (3 a.m. dread, tired but wired) with an analogy, then a fix | Videos 2, 17 |
| Study card | 15–25 s | One study on screen: who, what they did, what happened, what to try | Video 15, our to-do list script |
| Relatable moment | 7–15 s | A familiar night scene as the hook, with a caption that turns it into a tip | Videos 5, 8, 12 |
| "Me" caption | 7–12 s | A first-person caption over calm night visuals ("me finally getting out of bed instead of lying there") | BetterSleep's ads |

### Making volume cheap

- Write one hook and pair it with several visual sets. Each pairing is a separate post.
- Run recurring series with a numbered label, like "Night 2 of 7: the 3 a.m. wake-up." The quiz's five patterns make a natural series.
- Reuse the same technique across formats: a guided version, a myth version and a study card.
- Relatable moment and "me" caption videos follow the motion brief: no people, beds or phones on screen. Tell the moment with a super over a blurred night photo, like "Me after 'resting my eyes' for two hours."
- Every video ends on the sleep-check panel from the motion brief, with a "Take the sleep check" pill, after the voiceover loops back into the hook.

### Name the patterns from the quiz

Use the quiz's pattern names in hooks and captions: The Racing Mind, The 3 A.M. Waker, The Tired-but-Wired, The Shifted Clock, The Sleep Tryer. A viewer who recognizes themselves in "If you're a 3 A.M. Waker" is primed to take the quiz and see their result.

## Earning trust without a face

Most of the top videos leaned on a credential ("I'm a neurologist"). We can't, so we borrow authority from the method and the research instead, and never fake it.

- **Lead with CBT-I.** It's the first-line treatment for insomnia, ahead of pills, and most viewers have never heard of it. That surprise is our edge.
- **Put the source on screen.** A small line like "Scullin et al., 2018" or "American College of Physicians, 2016" under the claim.
- **Teach by doing.** A guided "try it with me" format feels like help, not a lecture.
- **Keep one consistent voice.** The same calm narrator and look every time, like a coach talking to a friend late at night.
- **Never fake credentials or experts.** No invented doctors, AI "experts" or made-up testimonials. If we want an expert line later, pay a clinician for a one-time review and credit them honestly.
- **Stay accurate.** Some viral videos used shaky claims (cortisol fixes, amygdala stories). We say "can help," not "cures," and check facts before posting.

## From competitor ads

Calm, BetterSleep and Rise all send ad clicks to a quiz, not a signup form, so our videos point to the NightShift sleep-check quiz too. Only the points that make content perform are kept here.

| Brand | What worked | What we take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm | Reframe hooks, an invite to join a group, a clear before-and-after over weeks, a quiz behind the click. Its quiz ads started on Sep 30, 2026, so they're still being tested | Reframe hooks. "Join the first group" framing in captions. Show what changes over a week |
| BetterSleep | Long-running "me listening to this sound…" captions in first person, with the visuals swapped between versions | The "me" caption format. One caption, many visuals |
| Rise | Quiz behind the click, side-by-side comparisons, AI-generated images | Simple comparisons (lying in bed vs getting up). No AI-generated people; the motion brief bans people on screen |

### Point every video at the quiz

- The caption ends with one line pointing to the quiz, for example "Take the 2-minute sleep check to find your pattern (link in bio)."
- Match the video to a quiz result. A 3 a.m. video points to "find out if you're a 3 A.M. Waker."
- The script itself never asks for a follow, share or click. The ask lives in the caption.

### What we don't copy

Fake tweets or testimonials, weight-loss or cortisol claims, tabloid-style shock headlines, countdown timers and fake discounts.

## Checklist before posting

Run every video through this list.

- [ ] The hook names a specific night-time situation or turns over a belief within 3 seconds
- [ ] The hook appears as on-screen text in the first frame, seven words or fewer
- [ ] It teaches one named technique or one new fact, not a list of tips
- [ ] There's one line of why, ideally with an everyday analogy
- [ ] The fix is concrete and doable tonight
- [ ] Any study or guideline is named on screen
- [ ] No clock-checking advice, no sleep scores, no supplement or medication advice
- [ ] No "cures" or "fixes insomnia"; say "can help"
- [ ] No attack on trackers, mattresses or anything viewers already own
- [ ] No fake experts, testimonials or credentials
- [ ] No "comment SLEEP" bait, follow asks or share asks in the script
- [ ] Caption ends with one line pointing to the quiz, matched to a pattern where possible
- [ ] Caption asks one specific question people can answer from experience
- [ ] Visuals match the NightShift motion brief: night photography, frosted glass, calm pacing
